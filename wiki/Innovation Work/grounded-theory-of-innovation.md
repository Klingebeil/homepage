# Grounded Theory of Innovation in Journalism

This is still a bit rough but the general idea is to sketch out an alternative approach to *innovation* then the dominant image presented by the tech-industry—which is still dominant in the media companies.

1. Incremental and Adaptive Change:
    - Innovation as a _slow, iterative process_ shaped by constraints like shrinking budgets, legacy systems, or editorial norms.
    - Change often occurs through _tinkering_, improvisation, or workarounds rather than through disruptive "breakthroughs."
2. Context-Specific Problem Solving:
    - Innovations are driven by _local newsroom needs_, not abstract notions of scalability or disruption.
    - For example, a small newsroom in a rural area might develop a new SMS-based reporting system—an innovation rooted in its own context, not in universal tech principles.
3. Cultural and Ethical Dimensions:
    - Innovation would be understood not just as a technical or business challenge but as an _ethical and epistemological_ one: How do new tools and formats affect trust, verification, and public service journalism?
4. Power, Labor, and Identity:
    - A grounded theory would surface the _power dynamics_ around innovation—who gets to define it, who gets funding, whose labor supports it (often women, freelancers, or junior staff), and who gets credit.        
5. Resistance and Non-Adoption:
    - It might also examine _resistance to innovation_ as a rational and meaningful choice, rather than framing it as backwardness or inertia.
