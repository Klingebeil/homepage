# Digital Business Folk Theories

These are narratives and ideas that are used in arguments about new technologies, are seldom questioned but broadly accepted as *truthful*. They’re often used as buzzwords.

- Killer Apps (pieces of software with a broad appeal) as the singular reason for the adoption of a technology (or a lack of a killer app being the reason for failed adoption)
- The Gartner Hype Cycle as a predictive model for technological development and adoption[^1]
- Early adopter advantage
- First mover advantage[^2]
- Resistance to change resulting from ignorance alone, not power relations or material conditions[^3]
- The popular understanding of Disruption as something inherent to technologies, not certain business strategies
- The popular notions of technological revolutions as clean cycles of technological innovation and adoption

[^1]:   See my notes on the [Gartner Hype Cycle (DE)](http://127.0.0.1:4000/2017/09/11/gartner-hypecycle.html)
[^2]:   Peter N. Golder, Gerard J. Tellis (1993). ‘Pioneer Advantage: Marketing Logic or Marketing Legend’. Journal of Marketing Research, Vol. 30, No. 2 (May, 1993), pp. 158-170. [http://www.jstor.org/stable/3172825](http://www.jstor.org/stable/3172825)
[^3]:   Jeffrey D. Ford, Laurie W. Ford, D'Amelio (February 06, 2008). ‘Resistance to Change: The Rest of the Story’. Academy of Management Review 2008, Vol. 33, No. 2, 362–377.
