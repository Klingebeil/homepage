# Why AI chat is a terrible interace for news

## Loss of Context and Depth
Chat interfaces inevitably simplify complex news stories. When a major global conflict gets reduced to “key takeaways” or “top 5 facts,” we lose crucial geopolitical history, conflicting perspectives, and systemic factors. 

This prioritization of brevity over comprehensiveness creates what researchers call *epistemic narrowing* — our understanding becomes constrained by the format itself.

## Hidden Decision-Making
Chat interfaces conceal how news is selected and presented. Users cannot easily determine if summaries contain bias, why certain sources were excluded, or whether corporate interests influenced the information. This lack of transparency — this “black-boxing” of editorial decisions—undermines accountability in news delivery, a cornerstone of democratic discourse.

## Discouraging Critical Engagement
Chat interfaces are optimized for convenience rather than reflection. News becomes a stream of digestible facts rather than material for deeper consideration. While a newspaper front page uses imagery and layout to convey the gravity of a humanitarian crisis, a chatbot might reduce this to a sentence or two.

The design promotes frictionless consumption over the resistance that often triggers critical thinking.

## Eroding Collective Discourse
News consumption has historically been partly public and social. Chat interfaces steer us toward private, individualized experiences that are customized and ephemeral.

This shift weakens shared discourse, making it harder to establish common ground for civic dialogue. As our news experiences diverge, filter bubbles deepen and collective understanding fragments.

## The Constructed “User”
When companies claim "users want news in chat form," they typically base this on engagement metrics that ignore qualitative aspects of experience. This approach treats people as consumers seeking convenience rather than citizens requiring substantive information.

We should question: Who is this “user”? Are they being viewed as critical thinkers or passive consumers? Do they genuinely prefer this format, or is it simply what's being offered?

## Co-Production of Behavior
Technologies and users shape each other. A reader who once engaged with long-form journalism gradually adapts to expecting only brief summaries. Their attention span, expectations, and trust in journalism are reconfigured by the interface. Saying "users want this" is ultimately like saying “fish want smaller tanks” — without acknowledging how the environment itself shapes desires and capabilities.

Rather than simply accepting AI chat as the future of news delivery, we should critically examine what's lost when we reduce journalism to a conversational interface. Better approaches might combine the accessibility of chat with the depth, transparency, and collective experience that traditional news formats can provide.
