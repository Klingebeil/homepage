# Pop-Futurism

A term coined by Richard Slaughter who had a healthy dislike of the corporate futurist speaker circle—or what can today simply be called: Linkedin.

*Pop-Futurism* in his own words:

> This is mostly trite, superficial work. It is media–friendly and can often be seen in weekend newspaper supplements, popular books and on brief TV features. **It is summed up by statements such as: “how science and technology are improving our lives and creating the future”. This is the world of the fleeting image and the transient sound–bite. It is eminently marketable, but largely bereft of theory or insight.**

Sounds familiar, eh?

He contrasts it with different, more grounded approaches, such as:
- *‌Problem–oriented futures work*—pretty much what it sounds like. Focussing on concrete near-term challenges to provide multiple options for action.
- *‌Critical and epistemological futures studies*—an even deeper questioning of ideas about the future itself, questions power structures and cultural paradigms, even ways we know and think.
