_Chronocentrism_ is the belief that one’s own time is the most important in history, with past and future periods seen as lesser. Chronocentrists view the present as the central turning point of human history.

Futurists and tech entrepreneurs like to make use of this notion, imagining the future mainly as a place to fix today’s problems rather than as a rich, independent era. 

>**An unfortunate effect of chronocentrism is a diminished appreciation of the future. In the way that the missionaries saw the Indians in restricted terms, so futurists often fail to conceive of the ripeness of life in the years to come. Due to their overestimation of the present, they see the future narrowly, as the place where the difficulties of the moment are worked out, and not as full blown in its own right.** Technology is a bothersome force for present day men, and so a prominent component of the imagined future international conflict troubles us, and so a future is pictured where it is played out.[^1]

Like ethnocentrism once justified imperialism, chronocentrism fuels the urge to dominate the future—locking tomorrow’s people into our infrastructure, politics, and values. 

[^1]: Fowles, J. (1974). On chronocentrism. *Futures*, 6(1), 65–68. https://doi.org/10.1016/0016-3287(74)90008-1
