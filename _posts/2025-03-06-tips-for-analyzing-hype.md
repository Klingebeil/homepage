### Never take claims at face value
Predictions are, at best, a well-informed opinion and, at worst, wild speculation. Never trust the hype, thought leadership and PR-speak.

### Look for limitations
Every technology has its limits. Look for points of failure, mistakes or breakage. These will give you a good idea of a technology’s realistic potential and where future progress might lead.

### Check the Motivation
What interests does your source have in amplifying hype? Are they trying to sell you something? Are they trying to change your mind? (e.g. “<i>VR is the future of work</i>”, says maker for home office furniture)

### Be a better news readeR
Pick industry reporting over broader tech journalism as your sources. Don’t fall prey to speculations.

### Get a historical perspective
What is the history of the technology in question? Most have been around way longer than the hype would like you to believe. Knowing their histories makes you harder to fool.

### Get an on-the-ground perspective
The expectations of people working on or with a given technology often differ from those selling it. Try to rely on their private assessments over the ones from keynote speakers.

### Re-Introduce Nuance
Absolute claims like “blockchain will revolutionize journalism” are excellent marketing but shoddy analyses. Start by asking yourself, “well, that depends on…?” and fill in the blanks. Poke holes, re-introduce nuance.

### Never dismiss critics
Despite boosters’ claims to the contrary, never dismiss people pointing out a technology’s limits or even dangers. Incorporate them into your analysis.

### Create alternatives
The future is not predetermined. On the contrary, it will be as diverse, messy, and complicated as the present. Try to formulate plausible alternative visions to challenge your assumptions.
