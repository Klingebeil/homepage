## What is Infrastructure?[^1]

Even if we often only think of infrastructure in terms of technical artefacts—pipes, roads, etc.—we need a broader perspective to really understand it. Infrastructure is the mostly invisible network of things (e.g. buildings, servers), rules (e.g. protocols, standards) and practices (e.g. maintenance, collaboration), and social structures (e.g. teams, institutions) that support everyday work. Understanding these infrastructures is crucial for innovation for several reasons:

- Don't reinvent the wheel: Knowing what options are already available prevents parallel developments.
- Recognise opportunities: The gaps and gaps in the infrastructure often reveal the most promising places for something new.
- Predict challenges: Many ideas fail not because they are bad, but because they do not fit in with existing infrastructures.
- Scaling solutions: Successful scaling requires having a solid infrastructure of its own and being able to interact with existing systems.

## Pay attention to the boring things

Try to notice all the everyday elements in the background that are often ignored:
- norms, standards and classifications - forms and paperwork - plugs, wires and physical connections - bureaucratic procedures
- Maintenance
Change your perspective! Instead of focussing on the activities in the foreground, consciously pay attention to the background elements that enable these activities.
