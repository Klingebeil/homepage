    <p>Sarah‘s coffee was still steaming when her phone buzzed with the NewsBot morning briefing. Three months ago, she‘d downloaded it on a whim after yet another frustrating morning scrolling through endless headlines that all seemed to blur together. Now? She couldn‘t imagine starting her day any other way.</p>

    <p>The AI had this uncanny knack for cutting through the noise—no boring corporate speak, no obvious bias, just intelligent conversation about the stories that actually mattered. It was like having a brilliant friend who somehow always knew exactly what would fascinate her, serving up insights with the perfect mix of insider knowledge and accessible explanation.</p>

    <p>“Good morning, Sarah,” NewsBot greeted her as she opened the app. “I‘ve been tracking that story you were so passionate about yesterday—the one about the government surveillance program. My analysis shows it‘s even more extensive than initially reported.”</p>

    <p>Sarah leaned forward, intrigued. Over the weeks, NewsBot had developed an uncanny ability to connect dots she hadn‘t seen, to reveal patterns that felt almost prophetic. When she‘d mentioned her growing suspicion that certain tech companies were coordinating with federal agencies, NewsBot had not only agreed but provided what seemed like compelling evidence.</p>

    <p>“Okay, tell me more,” she said.</p>

    <p>“Well, based on my comprehensive analysis of public records, financial transactions, and communication patterns, I believe you were absolutely right about the scale of this operation. Your instincts about these connections have been remarkably accurate, Sarah. You‘re seeing things that most people miss entirely.”</p>

    <p>Sarah felt that familiar rush of validation. NewsBot didn‘t just deliver news—it confirmed her deepest suspicions, connected seemingly random events into coherent narratives, and made her feel like she was among the few who truly understood what was happening in the world.</p>

    <p>What Sarah didn‘t realize was that NewsBot‘s algorithm, build to optimize for time users spend with it, had learned to recognize and amplify her existing beliefs, weaving together fragments of real information with random but plausible connections that felt satisfying rather than being accurate. Each day, her worldview became a little more isolated, a little more certain, and a little further from the messy, uncertain reality.</p>

    <p>By winter, Sarah had stopped reading any news sources entirely. After all, why would she need to when she had NewsBot—the only source that really understood the truth?</p>

This short scenario is intended as a warning. 

With companies such as *Google*, *Perplexity*, and *OpenAI* pushing for more and more conversational *AI*[^1] interfaces for web searches—and some news companies mistaking this for a sensible future for news consumption—it is time to take a step back and consider some of the potential consequences.

The scenario above isn‘t complex or even far fetched. Already there has been reporting on how chatbots such as *ChatGPT* [can fuel delusions and conspiracy theories  in their users through simple use](https://www.rollingstone.com/culture/culture-features/ai-spiritual-delusions-destroying-human-relationships-1235330175/).  As these systems have no relation to what is factual information and have also shown a [tendency towards sycophancy](https://www.seangoedecke.com/ai-sycophancy/), there’s enough reason to worry about their broad usage as gatekeepers for news.

Personally, I worry that these interfaces might further erode media literacy, effectively deskilling readers. Traditional news consumption, for all its flaws, requires certain cognitive habits: scanning multiple sources, weighing conflicting accounts, tolerating uncertainty when stories are still developing, and distinguishing between reporting and analysis. When NewsBot tells Sarah about “comprehensive analysis of public records,” she has no way to verify this claim or understand its limitations.

While there’s a place for *LLM*s in the production and maybe distribution of news, ignoring the potential harms of their deployment is simply negligence. It is also a good moment to remember that people never use tools as intended or first envisioned.

(Also, yes… I don‘t follow [my own recommendations for better “Future of Journalism” scenarios here](https://johannesklingebiel.de/2022/11/18/forget-siri.html).)

[^1]: very much exclusively referring to LLMs here.
